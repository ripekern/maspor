Codes and regulations

Code and Regulation No. 1: Guarding the gate

A shepherd that does not watch the way is not a shepherd, he is scenery. And there is a sentence in Nehemiah about men rebuilding a wall who were each given a second job on top of the work — a second job which was not building, and which they were never once allowed to put down. This is the counsel we give a household, so that what the world puts out on the air does not come in at the window. We do not command it, but we do press it — and we would rather a citizen heard the reason than simply obeyed the order.

Code and Regulation No. 1 · Our Citizens

A wall built in two hands

Here is a sentence about a building site, and it ought to stop anybody who thinks guarding a door is a soft subject. They were rebuilding the wall of Jerusalem — down for generations, and under threat the whole time they worked. So every man on that wall was given a second job, and the second job was not building.

He never told them to put the tools down. He told them to put down half of them. One hand works, the other hand holds — and then he counts it out, so that nobody may think it was a figure of speech: the builders, and the men that bare burdens, and the men that laded them, all of them working with one hand and holding a weapon with the other, and the rulers behind them all the way home.

“They which builded on the wall, and they that bare burdens, with those that laded, [every] [one] with one of his hands wrought in the work, and with the other [hand] held a weapon.”

Nehemiah 4:17

That is the whole of this code. The man on that wall was not suspicious of the city, and he was not hiding from it. He was at a job that had to be finished, in a place where finishing it could kill him — and so the guarding was never the alternative to the work. It was done in the other hand, while the first one worked.

We have all watched the other way of doing it. The tools go down on the coping, the hands go in the pockets, and everybody agrees the wall can wait. The wall cannot wait. It is the only reason anyone is still standing on it.

One hand builds and the other hand holds, and neither of them is ever put down. A wall is made that way or not at all, and a thing built that way is not a soft subject. So it is written down, and numbered, as the codes are: a code about this.

Why a code about this

We are not of the world. That is not a mood at us; it is the plain statement of who chose us and what we are set apart for. And the world does not come in by invitation. It comes in through the walls we did not know were thin, to people we love, at hours when we are asleep.

So the question is not whether a household ought to guard its door. Every honest man has a lock, and no one calls him a coward for having one. The question is what the lock is made of — and whether it is on the right door.

“Love not the world, neither the things [that] [are] in the world. If any man love the world, the love of the Father is not in him.”

1 John 2:15

“For all that [is] in the world, the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life, is not of the Father, but is of the world.”

1 John 2:16

“¶Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it [are] the issues of life.”

Proverbs 4:23

“Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart.”

2 Timothy 2:22

So we counsel every device in the household to have a content filter set at the router, and to have it checked afterwards. This is not a law and it is not a matter of taste either; it is what we believe to be right, and we set it down so that any man who thinks otherwise may answer us from the Word. What it consists of is plain enough, and it begins with what CleanBrowsing is.

What CleanBrowsing is

CleanBrowsing is a filtering DNS resolver. When a device asks it for the address of a website, it looks the name up in its own list and decides what to answer. For a blocked name it does not return an address at all, and the request simply never arrives. There is no account to create, nothing to install per device, and no logging of what our people read — the resolver answers the question it is asked and forgets it.

Setting it on the router is the important part. One entry at the router covers every device in the house at once — the laptops, the phones, the tablets, the television, the games console, and the things that were never asked about. Setting it on a single computer leaves every other door open, which is the same as not having set it.

“Abstain from all appearance of evil.”

1 Thessalonians 5:22

“Wherefore lay apart all filthiness and superfluity of naughtiness, and receive with meekness, the engrafted word, which is able to save your souls.”

James 1:21

One entry at the router covers every device in the house at once, and nothing is stored and nothing is logged of what our people read. Which of the three to set is the next question, and it is not a question of degree: which filter to choose.

Which filter to choose

There are three free filters, and the difference between them is not a matter of degree — it is a matter of what else they take with them. Take the family filter. It blocks adult content outright, and it also blocks the mixed-content sites, the proxy and VPN domains used to climb over a filter, and the malicious and phishing domains. It forces SafeSearch on the search engines. It costs nothing and it does the most.

The three free CleanBrowsing filters. All values below are taken from the provider’s own published settings.

Family 185.228.168.168 185.228.169.168 Adult content, mixed content, VPN and proxy domains, malware, phishing; SafeSearch enforced
Adult 185.228.168.10 185.228.169.11 Adult content, malware, phishing; leaves VPN and mixed-content sites alone
Security 185.228.168.9 185.228.169.9 Malware, phishing and spam only — no adult filtering whatsoever

The security filter is listed only so that it be understood what it is not. It will not do this work.

Take the family filter; it blocks the most and it costs nothing. Whichever one is chosen, it has to be set somewhere, and the best place to set it is setting it on the router.

Setting it on the router

Open the router’s admin page — almost always at 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1 — and sign in with the credentials printed on the router or set by whoever installed it. Find the DNS settings, which usually sit under WAN, Internet, or DHCP. Turn automatic DNS off, remove whatever is there, and enter the two addresses above. Save, and then restart the router so that its cache is cleared and every query begins at the new resolver.

If the network carries IPv6, it must be filtered too, or it becomes the way round the fence. Enter the family filter’s IPv6 addresses: 2a0d:2a00:1:: and 2a0d:2a00:2::. A household that filters IPv4 and leaves IPv6 open has not filtered anything.

The router covers the house — it does not cover the devices that leave the house.

On the devices that leave the house

The router covers the house. It does not cover a phone on a bar’s wifi, a tablet on a friend’s network, or anything at all once a cellular connection is used instead. For those, the filter has to be set on the device.

Android

Android 9 and later has a built-in slot for exactly this. Go to Settings → Network & internet → Private DNS, choose the private DNS provider hostname option, and enter family-filter-dns.cleanbrowsing.org. That is the whole of it — no app, no permission, nothing to maintain.

iPhone and iPad

Go to Settings → Wi-Fi, tap the i beside the network, then Configure DNS → Manual, delete the entries that are there, and add the two addresses. Note that this applies to one network only; it must be repeated for each network the device joins.

Windows

Open Settings, go to Network & internet, select the connection, and under DNS server assignment choose Edit, then Manual. Turn IPv4 on, enter 185.228.168.168 as the preferred address, add 185.228.169.168 as the alternate, and save.

macOS and Linux

On macOS the addresses go in under System Settings → Network → the connection → Details → DNS. On Linux, the nameservers are set in /etc/resolv.conf, or, where systemd-resolved is in use, in /etc/systemd/resolved.conf — in which case add the hostname alongside the addresses and set DNSOverTLS=yes, so that the queries are encrypted as well as filtered.

A phone on a bar’s wifi, a tablet on a friend’s network, anything at all once a cellular connection is used instead — for those the filter has to be set on the device. And then, whatever else is done, it has to be proved: check that it is actually working.

Check that it is actually working

A filter that was never applied is the commonest kind, and it is usually found by the person it was supposed to protect. Test it rather than assuming it. Open the provider’s own DNS leak test at cleanbrowsing.org/dnsleaktest and run it: the resolvers it reports must be CleanBrowsing addresses. Then try to reach a site that the filter blocks, and confirm that it does not come up. A leak test that reports anything other than 185.228.168.x or 185.228.169.x means the traffic is going somewhere else, and the setting has not taken.

“And if he shall come in the second watch, or come in the third watch, and find [them] so, blessed are those servants.”

Luke 12:38

“Watch ye therefore, and pray always, that ye may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that shall come to pass, and to stand before the Son of man.”

Luke 21:36

A filter that was never applied is the commonest kind, and it is usually the man it was meant to protect who finds it out. Run the leak test rather than assuming it. What none of it does is change the man standing behind the screen, and that is the next thing to be said plainly: what the fence does not do.

What the fence does not do

We will not oversell this. A filter is a fence, and a fence is not a root — it does not change the heart of the man standing behind it, and the man who wants through it will look for a way round, because there is always a way round. That is exactly why the Word is not optional here. The filter removes the occasion; it cannot give the strength to refuse it. A household that has a filter and no Bible has bought a delay.

It also does not make a man a citizen. Nothing does that but the new birth, and we have said so plainly on Our Citizens. The filter is housekeeping. It is worth doing — the wise built his house and then dug deeper — but it is not the foundation, and it is not a substitute for the Word, the prayer, and the look to the Shepherd that he has already given to those who are his.

“All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out. For I came down from heaven, not to do mine own will, but the will of him that sent me.”

John 6:37–38

“My little children, let us not love in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth.”

1 John 3:18

And so back to the wall, because that is where the story actually ends. When the builders had finished, Nehemiah did not send everybody home and call it a day. He set men on the gates and gave his order: they were not to be opened until the sun was high and the men standing at them had shut and barred them again. Then he appointed watches throughout the city — and gave each man a post over against his own house.

“And I said unto them, Let not the gates of Jerusalem be opened until the sun be hot; and while they stand by, let them shut the doors, and bar [them]: and appoint watches of the inhabitants of Jerusalem, every one in his watch, and every one [to] [be] over against his house.”

Nehemiah 7:3

The wall had a gate in it from the start. That is the point. A gate is where a wall is most a wall, because it is the only part of it a man can walk through — and a filter set on the router is precisely that: the one place in a household where the outside world is actually stopped, rather than merely noted. Nehemiah did not appoint anybody a hero. He appointed watches, and he gave every man a post, and the post in most houses is small and dull and entirely his own.

So let the filter be set, and let it be checked. But let the house be built on the Word first, and let nothing on this page be taken for a root of righteousness. The next counsel is not a wall at all; it is a coin. They sent a penny to catch him.
