Codes and regulations

Code and Regulation No. 3: No licence asked of the world

There is a way of enlisting the world in your obedience that costs nothing, asks no quarrel, and is signed for in an afternoon — and we counsel you to have nothing to do with it. A licence is a favour granted by the world, and what is granted may be withdrawn. We do not need a favour from Him who made heaven, earth, and sea. We do not ask permission to do the will of the Father.

Code and Regulation No. 3 · Our Citizens

Why a code about this

The world does not usually stop a man with a fist. It stops him with a counter, a queue, and a form. It asks whether he has registered; whether he has paid the fee; whether his name is on a list somewhere; whether a committee has seen fit to number him. And when he has answered, it gives him a piece of paper, and calls that piece of paper permission.

Nothing in that transaction belongs to God. And that is precisely the objection. To seek such a paper is to treat the world as the source of our standing — to go to the thing we were set apart from, and ask it for the very thing we were given. It is not merely paperwork. It is a transfer of allegiance, made in a font, at a counter, for a fee.

“Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish [ought] from it, that ye may keep the commandments of the LORD your God which I command you.”

Deuteronomy 4:2

“For the kingdom of God is not in word, but in power.”

1 Corinthians 4:20

“Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live:”

John 11:25

So we counsel our people to hold their citizenship from the King directly, and to let the world issue its papers without answering. This is not a law, and it is not a mood. It is what we believe to be right, and we set it down here so that any man who thinks otherwise may answer us from the Word.

We ask nobody’s permission

The God who made heaven, earth, and sea did not consult the world about how it should be run. He spoke, and it stood. He called light, and it answered. He made man in His own image, and did not apply to anyone for leave. When He brought His Son into the world, He did not file a petition. And when that Son says “as the Father hath sent me, and I also send you”, no earthly registry is made a party to the commission.

We do not need a licence from the world to do the will of the Father. That is not rudeness, and it is not a posture. It is arithmetic. A permission may be withdrawn by the one who grants it; a calling may not be withdrawn by anyone at all.

“God that made the world and all things therein, seeing that he is Lord of heaven and earth, dwelleth not in temples made with hands;”

On enfranchising with the world

We use an old word here, and we mean it precisely. To enfranchise is to bring somebody into a citizenship that is not your own — to make another’s polity your own, and to accept its liberties along with its obligations. We are not of the world, and we do not seek its franchise.

Registration is the usual door. It is not in itself sinful, and we will not say so: men are obliged to live in the world they are in, and a man may pay a tax he thinks unjust and not thereby sell his soul. But we will counsel you to know which door you came through. A registry that records what you own does not trouble us. A registry that decides who you are has asked a question that was never its to ask — and if your standing in your own kingdom rests on its answer, then you are not the citizen of the King first. You are the client of a clerk.

The same is true of a licence. A licence is a permission to do what another has allowed. There is a kind of work that is freely given and never once applied for, and a man who cannot tell the difference between the two has been made dependent without noticing it.

What this looks like on an ordinary week

None of this asks a man to hide, or to suffer a fine, or to leave his work. We counsel that he should simply stop treating the world’s paperwork as the foundation, and carry it as what it is.

We have drawn the line where Scripture drew it: not at what a licence is, but at where the standing rests.
The thing What it is What we counsel
A licence to trade A permission from a local authority to sell Obey the law while it stands, and let no licence decide whether you are counted among God’s
A vehicle registration A record of what you own, so you may be charged for it Pay what is owed; do not let a plate stand for your standing
A company, partnership or LLC A legal form, which is a filing Use it if you must; never take your name from it
A church register, or a public worship An earthly roll of names Go in; but hold your citizenship from the King and not from the clerk’s book
A mark, permit or consent “for the public good” The world’s word that your doing is approved Do the will of the Father and neither seek that word nor fear its absence

The last row is the one that matters. None of the rest decides who you are; only that one was ever pretending to.

What this code does not say

We will not overstate this. It does not make a man a citizen, and it does not keep one from falling. It cannot do either. Citizenship is the new birth, and nothing a man files or declines to file has any part in it; we have said so plainly on Our Citizens. Judas held an office under a licence from men and lost it anyway, and the loss was nothing to do with paperwork. A man may hold every paper the world offers and still be lost; a man may hold none and be kept.

Nor does it make a man a martyr. We counsel no conflict with the authorities. The law of the place is to be obeyed in its lawful measure, and a man does not wound his own cause by a needless contest. What is refused is not the law but the worship — the one thing that was never the world’s to give, and so was never the world’s to withhold.

“They say unto him, Cæsar’s. Then saith he unto them, Render therefore unto Cæsar the things which are Cæsar’s; and unto God the things that are God’s.”

Matthew 22:21

“And when they had prayed, the place was shaken where they were assembled together; and they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and they spake the word of God with boldness.”

Acts 4:31

“Who gave himself for us, that he might redeem us from all iniquity, and purify unto himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works.”

Titus 2:14

So let the papers be what they are, and let the standing be where it belongs. This code is housekeeping, like the one before it. The root is the Word, and the name in that Word, and none of it is filed anywhere.
